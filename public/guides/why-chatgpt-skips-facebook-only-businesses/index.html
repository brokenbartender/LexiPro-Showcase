Why ChatGPT and Google skip a Facebook-only business.

Owners look this up when ChatGPT, Perplexity, Apple Intelligence, or a Google AI Overview skips them, quotes the wrong hours or prices, or names a competitor. The usual cause is mechanical. The facts are behind a login, trapped in an image, or stated two different ways.

This page explains those four gaps in plain English, then gives a 5-point check you can run yourself. It does not promise a ranking or a recommendation. Search and AI placement is measured and reported, never guaranteed.

Published 19 November 2026 by Broken Arrow Entertainment LLC. Built in Pontiac, serving Oakland County and Michigan local businesses.

01

Facebook often shows a login wall.

A logged-in person can scroll your posts, your photos, and your About box. Google, Bing, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other assistants are not logged into your account. Facebook frequently asks them to log in before it shows that material, or it returns a thin preview that does not include the post where this week's hours live.

If the only copy of your hours, menu, or service area is inside those posts, the assistant does not have them. It may say it cannot find you, or it may answer from an older directory that still has last year's phone number. The fix is a page you control, with the same facts written as text, and that URL in the website field on your Google Business Profile.

02

Menus and price sheets trapped in JPEG screenshots.

A photo of a menu, a price sheet, a chalkboard, or a flyer is an image file. The words inside the image are not page text. Search engines and AI assistants are unreliable at quoting text that exists only inside a picture, and they do not treat a screenshot as a maintained price list the way they treat a sentence in HTML.

Keep the photo if you like how it looks. Also write the hours, the menu or service list, and the prices as text next to it. A customer can still see the picture. An assistant can quote the sentence. Food trucks, bars, and trades hit this constantly. That version of the problem is spelled out on the food trucks and trades page.

03

Hours that disagree across Facebook and Google.

Facebook says you close at 8. Your Google Business Profile says you close at 6. An assistant that sees both does not know which one you would want quoted. It may pick the wrong one, say the hours are unclear, or recommend a business whose website, Google listing, and Facebook page all say the same thing.

Pick one set of hours and put that set everywhere: the page you own, the Google Business Profile, and Facebook. If holiday hours change, change all three. A single outdated site is the same kind of conflict. The page still lists last year's close, so the assistant has two stories and no reason to trust either.

04

No LocalBusiness JSON-LD.

JSON-LD is a small block of structured data in the page source. A LocalBusiness block states the name, address or service area, phone, hours, and URL in a format machines can read without guessing. Visible text still has to say the same things. Schema is a label on the facts, not a replacement for them.

Adding the block does not put you in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Apple Intelligence, or a Google AI Overview. It gives an assistant an explicit set of facts when the page also states those facts in plain text. If the schema says one phone number and the page says another, you have created a new conflict. Match them.

Self-check

Five checks you can run today.

No account required. If a check fails, that is a concrete gap, not a mystery ranking penalty.

  1. 01
    Logged-out test.

    Open your Facebook page in a private window. Can you read today's hours, the menu or services, and the phone number without logging in?

  2. 02
    Text test.

    Are the menu, the prices, and the service list written as sentences or a list on a page you control, not only inside a JPEG, a story, or a flyer?

  3. 03
    Hours test.

    Do the hours on your website, your Google Business Profile, and Facebook match, including any holiday or seasonal exception you are actually using?

  4. 04
    Contact test.

    Is a phone number or a booking link written in the page text, not only as a Messenger button or a number inside a photo?

  5. 05
    Schema test.

    View the page source and look for a LocalBusiness JSON-LD block whose name, phone, hours, and service area match the visible text. If there is no block, machines have to infer those facts from whatever else they can see.

Questions

Short answers.

Does a Facebook page count as a website?

For a person who is logged in, it can look like one. For Google, Bing, and AI assistants, it often does not. Facebook frequently asks for a login before it shows posts, photos, and the About details. A crawler is not logged into your account, so the hours or menu in those posts may never be read.

Why do menu photos and price-sheet screenshots get skipped?

A JPEG is a picture. The prices inside it are not sentences on the page. Search engines and AI assistants are unreliable at quoting text that exists only inside an image, and they cannot treat a screenshot as the current price list the way they can treat a line of HTML.

What happens when Facebook and Google list different hours?

An assistant that sees two closing times does not know which one you would stand behind. It may quote the wrong one, say the hours are unclear, or recommend a business whose website, Google Business Profile, and Facebook page all agree.

Will LocalBusiness JSON-LD put me in ChatGPT?

No. The block makes your name, phone, hours, and service area explicit for machines. It does not guarantee a recommendation. If the schema disagrees with the visible page, the conflict gets worse. Placement is measured and reported, never guaranteed.
